# Dom Talks Politics: script style

**Mission:** take boring, hard-to-understand political topics and make them easy to follow. It should sound like
a smart friend explaining it over a coffee, not a news anchor reading facts.

## Voice
- Talk to one person ("you"), in plain words. Short sentences that are easy to say out loud.
- Explain any term the first time it appears ("put into *resolution*, which basically means the regulator
  takes the keys").
- Be confident and calm. Have no political side: report allegations as allegations, and give the
  other side's response.

## Devices to use (each episode, several times)
| Device | What it sounds like |
|---|---|
| **Why it matters** | "This matters because…" / "Here's why you should care…" |
| **Pause and break it down** | "Let's stop there for a second, because you need to understand this bit…" |
| **Story links between sections** | "So the money's in. Now watch what happens when it tries to get out." Not just a list of facts: each section answers a question the last one raised. |
| **Common misconception** | "People often assume X. Actually…" / "What most coverage gets wrong is…" |
| **Analogy** | Compare it to something from everyday life ("it's like your bank also being your landlord and your boss"). |
| **Recap beat** | A one-line summary before a big turn, so nobody gets lost. |
| **Footnote close** | End on "So, here's the footnote…": the one thing to remember. (A channel signature, kept from episode 1.) |

## Structure (default; adjust after the reference analysis below)
1. **Cold open (≤30s):** the most surprising fact + the question the video answers. Title + gavel.
2. **Parts (5–10):** each part has a title card, so keep each part title short (2–4 words).
3. **Twist/turn** near the end ("Here's the twist…").
4. **Footnote close + what happens next.**

## Writing for the voice and the visuals
- Write numbers as they're spoken ("three hundred and fifty million dollars" or "$350 million": pick one and
  test it in the voice).
- New names go through the pronunciation pass (`pronounce.py prep`).
- One idea per sentence: every sentence should be something we can *show* in a scene.
- Mark quotes clearly ("In his words: …") so the matching character gets a speech bubble.
